Play with a purpose

Classroom games

These games are included in the 45-minute lesson timings above.

5 min · Warm-up

Fact or inference?

Prepare: Write the umbrella sentence on the board. Each pair uses F and I cards, or writes F or I on paper.

Play: Read “Nomsa has an umbrella” (fact), “It may have been raining” (inference), and “Nomsa dislikes rain” (unsupported). Pairs choose a label and explain their evidence. Give one point for each correct, justified answer; discuss unsupported guesses.

Check learning: Ask each learner to explain the difference between a stated fact and an inference.

12 min · Pair work

Evidence detectives

Prepare: Use the market-stall passage and questions 1–3. Write two inference cards: “Lindiwe expected rain” and “Lindiwe wanted to help the other vendor.”

Play: After answering the questions, pairs match each card to a clue: covering the bags before customers arrived; handing over the spare sheet. Swap detective and explainer roles. A match earns one point only when both partners explain the connection.

Check learning: Each pair reads one inference with its supporting clue. A seated, spoken version works without printed cards.

Teacher preparation

Foreseen challenges & responses

What to anticipate

Learners may confuse inference with guessing or struggle to select evidence.

How to respond

Model a think-aloud, highlight the relevant clue and offer sentence starters. Let pairs rehearse before independent writing.

Materials: Board and one worksheet per learner or pair.

Student worksheet

Name: ___________________________ Date: ______________

The market stall

Clouds gathered over the market. Lindiwe moved the bags of maize under the table and pulled a sheet over them. When the first customers arrived, drops were running off the edge of the sheet. The bags were dry. Across the path, a vendor was lifting a damp box off the ground. Lindiwe handed him the spare sheet she had folded under her chair.

  1. Where did Lindiwe put the maize bags?
  2. Copy a detail that shows it had started to rain.
  3. Why did Lindiwe move the bags and cover them? Give one clue.
  4. What does the last sentence suggest about Lindiwe? Support your answer with evidence.
  5. Rewrite this as an inference supported by evidence: “Lindiwe was prepared.”

Teacher answer key

  1. Under the table.
  2. “Drops were running off the edge of the sheet.”
  3. She wanted to keep the maize dry; she covered the bags before the drops began, and they remained dry.
  4. She was helpful: she gave the other vendor her spare sheet when his box was damp.
  5. Accept a justified answer, e.g. “I infer Lindiwe had planned for bad weather because she already had a spare sheet folded under her chair.”

For questions 3–5, award one point for a reasonable inference and one for relevant evidence.
